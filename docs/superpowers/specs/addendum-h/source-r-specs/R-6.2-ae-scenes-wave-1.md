# R-6.2 AE scene set wave 1 (10 scenes): AR/EN stills, read-back, face keep-out

Phase 6 (first wave may start in Phase 4 on the looks track). Owners: looks track (`wnc_motion_lib.jsx` builders,
look data, `skills/motion-craft/references/motion-scenes.md`), executor (new scene types in `motion_script.py` /
`motion_plan.py` validation, `ae_builder.py`), Adobe (read-back ops if new ones are needed).

## Source-R behaviour (shared contract of its motion concepts)

- Target 1080×1920. Beats are fractions of the scene length.
- Two modes: a play-once mode (ends in a frozen, readable hold) and a looping mode (returns to
  the opening state).
- Each scene has a minimum length defined at 30 fps and scaled by fps/30.
- Strict per-field copy limits; text that does not fit is rejected, never clipped.
- Demo data is labelled as a sample.
- Scenes draw graphics only and never locate the face; the caller must keep them off the face.
- There is no dedicated test suite for the scenes.

## Our version (shared contract for every scene below)

1. **Anchored to speech.** A scene starts at its `at` (2–12 transcript words, `motion-scenes.md`); elements inside it
   appear at their own spoken `at` where they have one.
2. **Duration from reading time**, not fixed frames (closed form, seconds):
   `base = entrance + words_on_screen / reading_rate`; `hold = max(0.8, 0.3 · base)` (so the hold is about 23 % of the
   scene; a starting value of ours, measured on cold-viewer reads before a look leaves `preview_gate`). `entrance` = the look's law entrance time of the first element + its stagger × (elements − 1), in
   seconds (stagger counts; elements that appear on their own spoken `at` do not add to it). `reading_rate` = 2.5
   words/s for Arabic and 3.0 for English: **starting values**, measured on our client reels (on-screen reading of
   cold viewers) before the first look leaves `preview_gate`, recorded with their basis in `motion.json` (through
   the executor);
   `duration = max(recipe_min, base + hold)`, where `recipe_min` is the content type's minimum scene seconds in
   `motion-beats.md` (Premiere overlay reels with no motion recipe use 2.5 s). When the speech anchors give the scene
   more time (the next scene's `at` is later), the hold grows. The recipe's **max** scene seconds applies to the
   **reading-time length** (`base + hold`) only: if that alone exceeds the max, `reel motion-plan` returns a plan error
   naming the scene and the field that carries the most words, with the fix ("قسّم المشهد لاتنين أو قلّل البنود") — it
   never auto-splits and never speeds the reading. A scene that is longer only because its spoken items are spread out
   is allowed, with a plan warning ("المشهد طويل لأن الكلام متباعد"). No loop mode in 3.0 (reels are linear); loops arrive with no-recording
   motion videos in 3.1.
2b. **Ordering**: scenes stay ordered and non-overlapping (existing plan validation); a scene listed in a look's
   `exclusive_scenes` (R-6.1) is refused under other looks.
3. **Fit-or-fail with the real font**, per field, at the look's sizes (HarfBuzz / CoreText); word caps per field come
   from the recipe; code-point caps are not used. "too long: split" errors name the field.
4. **No sample data.** Every value comes from the transcript, the evidence words, the client database or the editor.
   A scene whose data is illustrative is labelled «توضيحي» / "illustrative" on screen automatically.
5. **Truth rules** of `motion-scenes.md` (speech vs editorial text, numbers' digits must be said, people via
   `{host}`/`{guest}`); numbers and claims pass claim-checker; medical/religious statements pass medical-reviewer.
6. **Look-driven.** Colours, type, cards, annotation and motion come from the active look (R-6.1); nothing hard-coded.
7. **RTL first.** Direction rules of `motion-beats.md` § "Direction (RTL vs LTR)" ("next" moves toward the reading end; first panel on the right
   in Arabic); Arabic animated by word or line only.
8. **Native and editable.** Each scene builds named AE layers (and, for Premiere overlay use, a MOGRT with Essential
   Graphics text/colour/timing properties).
9. **Checked.** Every AE build first runs the existing Arabic capability probe (`ae_probe.py`: Middle Eastern text
   engine, RTL paragraph direction); if it fails, Arabic scenes are refused with the fix line and English scenes still
   build. Each scene has an AR and an EN still per 3.0 look, a read-back (layer boxes, text, timing) compared with the
   plan, the safe-zone profile check (R-4.5), the head keep-out gate (R-6.4), SFX cue kinds (R-6.7) and the R-4.1
   font chain + missing-glyph check. Budget: one scene build ≤ 20 s in AE, render excluded (a starting value,
   measured; over budget = a warning).
10. **Tests.** A scene test per builder (plan validation, fit-or-fail, RTL mirror, read-back fields) — the suite
    Source-R does not have.

Our scene names are our own. Each block: **Source-R** (behaviour) → **Our version** (the seven R2 points).

### 1. `hero_word` — a single key word lands solid, sends out an echo, then a line under it
**Source-R.** A hero word appears as an outline, fills with colour, then a sub-headline fades in.

**Our version.**
1. Name: `hero_word`.
2. Motion: the word arrives **solid** at its spoken time by a mask reveal in the reading direction (8 f, ease-out
   80 %); two outline echoes of the same word then expand from it (scale 100 % → 118 %, opacity 60 % → 0, 14 f, the
   second 4 f later), like a pulse; the line under it comes in by the look's law at its own spoken `at`. No
   outline-then-fill sequence.
3. Brand: the word in ink or brand primary (contrast-stepped), the echoes in brand accent.
4. Arabic: **whole Arabic words only** (no per-glyph animators); the echo is a duplicate of the shaped word layer
   with a stroke, so joins are never broken; a Latin term inside stays one LTR run.
5. Easier: the motion-director picks it for a hook word or a named concept; the word is the speaker's own.
6. Quality: fit-or-fail on the word at the look's display size (shrink floor 85 %); the echo's 118 % size must stay
   inside the safe-text rectangle (checked); the line under it is editorial or speech, marked by its `_source` field.
7. Distinctiveness: solid-first with an expanding echo pulse; nothing of the outline → fill order.

### 2. `card_fan` — 3–5 cards fan out from a stack
**Source-R.** Five tool cards fan out from a single stack; each card has a name, a function line and an optional
icon.

**Our version.**
1. Name: `card_fan`.
2. Motion: the stack lifts 12 px, then cards fan along an arc toward the reading end, each on its spoken `at` (or 4 f
   apart when the items are said together), ease-out by the look's law; the last card settles with no overshoot.
3. Brand: card plate = look card role; icons in brand primary; names in ink.
4. Arabic: the fan opens right → left in Arabic; card text right-aligned.
5. Easier: 3–5 cards (Source-R is fixed at 5); icons from the Lucide index or the client's logo files.
6. Quality: each card's name and line fit-or-fail; icons are licensed (Lucide ISC) or the client's own.
7. Distinctiveness: an arc fan with our card radius and timing per spoken item, not a fixed five-card spread.

### 3. `tile_focus` — a small grid of numbers; the stressed one widens in place and rolls to its value
**Source-R.** A 5-tile dashboard (name, value, change %); one tile zooms to a hero with a count-up; an optional
unit.

**Our version.**
1. Name: `tile_focus`.
2. Motion: 2–5 tiles appear by the look's law; at the focus `at` there is **no camera zoom**: the focus tile widens
   in place to the full grid width (12 f, ease-out 75 %) while the other tiles slide out toward the reading-start
   edge; its value **rolls digit by digit** like an odometer (each digit column rolls vertically to its final digit),
   by place value: **units first, then tens, then hundreds…**, 3 f apart, 10 f each, in both languages (numbers are
   written left to right with either digit set).
3. Brand: tiles on the card surface; the focus tile's value in brand accent.
4. Arabic: tile order right → left; Arabic-Indic or Western digits per client (the roll uses the chosen digit set);
   the % sign follows the digit set.
5. Easier: tiles come from numbers the speaker says (stat evidence); fewer than 5 is fine.
6. Quality: **every value is source-verified** (digits must be in the evidence words; otherwise the editor confirms;
   claim-checker for claims); the roll always ends on the exact said value and never passes through a larger one.
7. Distinctiveness: in-place widening and a digit roll instead of a zoom with a count-up.

### 4. `step_path` — a pulse runs a path through numbered steps
**Source-R.** A pulse runs along an S-shaped path: an input, 4 numbered steps and an output badge.

**Our version.**
1. Name: `step_path`.
2. Motion: the path trim-draws at constant speed; each step node activates (scale 90 % → 100 %, 6 f) as the speaker
   says it; a soft pulse travels the drawn part only.
3. Brand: path in brand primary, active node in brand accent, labels in ink.
4. Arabic: **the path flows right → left in Arabic** (and left → right in English); numbers on the reading-start side
   of each node.
5. Easier: 2–5 steps from the transcript (`items[{text, at}]`, like `list_steps`); input/output optional.
6. Quality: an extension of our `timeline` scene geometry; each label fit-or-fail; the path avoids the head envelope
   (it reroutes to the alternate layout when the face sits in its band).
7. Distinctiveness: a zig-zag on our 8-column grid with node timing from speech, not a fixed S-curve with 4 steps.

### 5. `bars_to_trend` — bars grow, then become a trend line and land on the said result
**Source-R.** Six bars grow, then morph into a line, then the final KPI appears verbatim with a result tag (a metric
label, the values, the final KPI, a result label, an optional unit).

**Our version.**
1. Name: `bars_to_trend`.
2. Motion: 3–8 bars grow from the baseline in reading order (4 f apart, ease-out 75 %); the bar tops connect into a
   path (trim 12 f); the bars fade to 25 % and the final value lands at its spoken `at`.
3. Brand: bars in brand primary at 60 %, the path and the final value in brand accent.
4. Arabic: time runs right → left in Arabic; digits per client.
5. Easier: values from the speaker's numbers or the client's data file; the scene refuses invented series.
6. Quality: **numbers must be source-verified** (claim-checker); the axis is honest (zero baseline unless the editor
   approves otherwise, stated in the plan).
7. Distinctiveness: a fade-to-ghost bars + path reveal instead of a morph, our bar radius and spacing.

### 6. `before_after` — two images with a wipe
**Source-R.** Before/after images with exact dimensions, labels, a reveal frame and duration, and a direction (rtl,
ltr, up, down).

**Our version.**
1. Name: `before_after`.
2. Motion: a linear wipe (AE Linear Wipe, feather 24 px) in the chosen direction, eased by the look's law; the divider
   line rides the wipe edge.
3. Brand: labels on brand-paper tags; divider in brand accent.
4. Arabic: the wipe is **progress** (direction rule): it runs from the reading start to the reading end, so in
   Arabic the "after" image is revealed starting at the right edge and the wipe moves right → left.
5. Easier: images picked from the client database or the editor's files; labels default to «قبل / بعد» or
   "Before / After".
6. Quality: both images must have the same pixel size (checked; otherwise a fit crop is proposed); medical before/after
   images need medical-reviewer approval and the client's consent flag `consents.before_after_images: true` in the
   reel-profile (set by the editor; missing = no consent, the plan refuses the scene).
7. Distinctiveness: our feathered wipe with a riding divider and tag labels.

### 7. `dual_dial` — two values as two arcs on one shared dial, with the source line
**Source-R.** Two values on the same positive maximum, with a unit and a real source label.

**Our version.**
1. Name: `dual_dial`.
2. Motion: a single half-dial (a 180° arc track) draws in (trim path, 10 f); the two values sweep as two concentric
   arcs on that same dial (outer = first value, inner = second), the second starting 6 f after the first (ease-out
   14 f); each value's number rides the end of its arc and settles by a digit roll (as `tile_focus`).
3. Brand: the dial track in muted, the arcs in brand primary and accent, the labels in ink.
4. Arabic: the arcs sweep from the right end of the dial in Arabic, from the left in English (progress rule); the
   unit sits **after the number in reading order** (left of it in Arabic: «٤٠٪» stored digits then ٪ with no space,
   shaped as one run; «٣ مليون»), never
   split from it; Latin units (kg, mg) stay a Latin run after the number.
5. Easier: made from "X vs Y" statements the story-editor finds.
6. Quality: **one shared maximum by construction** (one dial; two scales are impossible); **the source line is
   mandatory** and comes from the evidence (claim-checker); a unit is required.
7. Distinctiveness: a shared half-dial with concentric arcs and riding numbers, not two parallel meters.

### 8. `speaker_quote` — an exact quote anchored to the speaker who said it
**Source-R.** A chapter, a title, an exact quote, the speaker, the role and a quote reveal frame.

**Our version.**
1. Name: `speaker_quote` (extends our `quote_card`).
2. Motion: the look's law (LOOK-10: `ML-SEAT`: the name chip grows from the speaker's seat side; the quote band dims
   and blurs the speaker frame in AE, and is a solid brand-paper band at 88 % in Premiere overlays; lines fade up).
3. Brand: name chip and topic bar in brand primary; quote text in paper over the dimmed band (AE) or in ink on the
   solid band (Premiere), contrast-stepped.
4. Arabic: «» quote marks in Arabic; attribution on the reading-end side.
5. Easier: topic from the episode topic map; name, role and seat side from the episode sheet and speaker turns
   (`{host}`/`{guest}`).
6. Quality: the quote is **exact transcript** (the motion-plan truth rule: at most 40 % left out, nothing added);
   checked again after the time map.
7. Distinctiveness: seat-anchored chip + dimmed-band pull-quote (R-6.1 LOOK-10), no numbered chapter or quote rule.

### 9. `kinetic_quote` (upgrade) — literal recorded words by index
**Source-R.** Animated type built from literal recorded word indices, with a language, an entrance, per-word
emphasis, a kicker and a title; it checks the words against the cut.

**Our version.**
1. Name: our existing `kinetic_quote`, upgraded.
2. Motion: the look's law (LOOK-03: `ML-PUNCH`, word arriving at its spoken time).
3. Brand: emphasis words in brand highlight (contrast-stepped).
4. Arabic: word-level only; lines broken by measured width; mixed AR/EN runs isolated.
5. Easier: the scene can be authored as `word_ids` (indices into `words.json`) or as text; text is resolved to ids.
6. Quality: **ids are validated against `words.json` after the time map** (a cut word cannot be shown; a missing id
   is a plan error naming it).
7. Distinctiveness: our word-arrival law and line balance; no kicker/title stack by default.

### 10. `stack_focus` — the speaker's points build a stack, then the stressed one opens while the rest fold to chips
**Source-R.** 3–6 items with stable IDs gather from scattered positions into a board, then one item moves forward,
with minimum readable holds before gathering, on the full board and after the focus.

**Our version.**
1. Name: `stack_focus`.
2. Motion: there is **no scatter**: each item drops into a vertical stack on its spoken `at` (the look's law), so the
   stack builds as the speaker talks; at the focus `at` the other items **fold into small chips** in a row along the
   bottom of the slot (scale Y to a chip, 10 f, ease-out 75 %) while the focus item **opens in place to a full card**
   (2D scale to fill the slot width, 12 f; no Z move). Holds: 1.0 s on the full stack, 1.2 s after the focus.
3. Brand: items on the card surface; the chips in muted; the open card framed in brand accent.
4. Arabic: the stack aligns to the reading start; the chips line up right → left in Arabic.
5. Easier: items are the speaker's own points (`items[{text, at}]`); the focus is the one the speaker stresses.
6. Quality: **object continuity**: the same AE layers live through all states (no swap to new layers), so the editor
   can re-time it by moving keys; the read-back checks the focus card's final scale (2D) and the chips' row.
7. Distinctiveness: a speech-built stack that folds to chips, instead of a scatter that gathers into a board.

## SFX cues each scene emits (R-6.7 kinds; one marker per cue)

| Scene | Cues |
|---|---|
| `hero_word` | `hit` when the word lands; `appear` for the line under it |
| `card_fan` | `appear` per card |
| `tile_focus` | `appear` for the grid; `exchange` when the focus tile widens; `tally` when the value settles |
| `step_path` | `tick` per step node |
| `bars_to_trend` | `appear` for the bars; `tally` when the final value lands |
| `before_after` | `exchange` at the wipe |
| `dual_dial` | `appear` for the dial; `tally` per value |
| `speaker_quote` | `appear` for the name chip; none for the quote lines |
| `kinetic_quote` | `keys` per arriving word only when the look's palette maps `keys` (off by default) |
| `stack_focus` | `tick` per item; `exchange` at the focus |
Scene changes add `scene_change`; a look's logo moment adds `logo`. Nothing in the protected sign-off span (R-4.7).

## Acceptance (R-6.2)
For each of the 10 scenes: plan validation tests (fields, truth rules, fit-or-fail naming the field), an RTL mirror
test and the direction-rule test (00-INDEX), a duration-formula test (base, hold, recipe_min, the over-max error and the
speech-spread warning), a digit-roll test (the shown value never passes the final value and ends on it, both digit
sets), duration floors scaled by fps, AR and EN stills for each 3.0 look viewed by the frame-inspector, read-back
fields compared with the plan, the safe-zone and head keep-out checks, and a distinctiveness verdict from the
brand-systems-designer. `motion-scenes.md` lists each scene with its fields and text sources.
