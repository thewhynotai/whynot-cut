# R-4.2 Burned-in caption detection: hide our captions, keep the words

Phase 4, Task 24. Owner: executor (engine). Reuses `overlay_scan.py` (G65) ideas; caption looks (R-4.1) consume the
result.

## Source-R behaviour

- A per-video switch hides the caption overlay when the source footage already carries burned-in subtitles.
- The words stay in the edit: cuts, fillers, retakes and word-timed quote scenes still use them.
- The switch is set **by the operator** (the assistant or the creator), not detected.

## Our version

### What it does
`reel intake` (and `reel plan` on footage that changed) **detects** burned-in captions automatically and, when they
are found, sets the reel's caption visibility to hidden by default, keeps every word for cuts and the transcript, and
tells the editor in one line. The editor can turn captions back on with one switch (panel or `--captions on`).

### Detection (OCR-free first, measured)
1. Sample frames (320×180 thumbnails at 1 per second, `overlay_scan.decode_thumbs`, GPU decode when available via
   Task 10's NVDEC / VideoToolbox, CPU fallback):
   - **Podcast episodes**: the episode intake already decodes these thumbnails for `overlay_scan` (before
     transcription); the band test runs on the **same** thumbnails in that pass, and its per-second band results are
     kept; once a reel window is picked and transcribed, the results inside that window are mapped onto its speech
     ranges. No second decode.
   - **Single clips** (`reel intake` has no overlay scan today): their own 1 fps pass over the clip, with a small
     budget (default 20 s of wall time; clips are short), started after transcription so the speech ranges exist.
   - It always runs in the background; an unfinished scan gives `complete: false` and the verdict `maybe`.
   - **Late result**: if the verdict arrives after `reel plan` or a build already ran, nothing is changed
     automatically; the editor gets a notice with the one switch («لقينا ترجمة محروقة في الفيديو؛ تحب نخبّي الكابشن
     بتاعنا؟»). The automatic hide applies only when the verdict exists before the plan is compiled (G10: never blocks,
     slows or silently rewrites).
2. Text-band candidates: the lower 45 % and upper 20 % of the frame are searched for horizontal bands with **high
   short-edge density** (glyph strokes: many short vertical and horizontal edges in a band 3–12 % of the frame height)
   and a **high-contrast core** (light text with a dark rim, or dark text on a light plate).
3. Speech rhythm test (what separates captions from lower-thirds and logos): a caption band **changes content** at a
   rate that follows the speech (band-content changes correlate with cue boundaries, Pearson ≥ 0.4 over the
   sampled span; at 1 sample per second the test uses cue boundaries, not single words) and is **absent during long pauses**. A lower-third holds still for seconds and does not follow the
   words; a logo never changes; both fail the rhythm test.
4. Verdict per reel: `burned_in: yes` when ≥ 60 % of sampled speech seconds carry a band that passes both tests,
   `maybe` between 25 % and 60 % (the editor is asked in one line, nothing changes), `no` below 25 %.
5. Evidence: a 6-tile contact sheet of the strongest frames goes to `08_quality_control/` so the intake-inspector and
   the editor can see why.

### Effects
- `burned_in: yes` → the caption look's visibility becomes `hidden` for this reel (edit-decisions, not the profile),
  the cue file is still written, captions-short still exists, word-timed quote scenes and cut proposals still use the words.
- The editor sees: «الفيديو فيه ترجمة محروقة في الصورة، فخبّينا الكابشن بتاعنا عشان ميبقاش فيه اتنين. الكلام لسه موجود
  للقص.» (one line, AR or EN by the editor's language).
- Graphics placement treats the detected band as an avoid zone (so a statement bar does not cover the burned text).

### Better than Source-R
Automatic instead of a manual switch; the band also becomes an avoid zone; evidence frames are shown; it never blocks.

## Inputs → outputs
| Input | Output |
|---|---|
| main footage, `words.json` | `01_analysis/burned-in-captions.json` `{verdict, coverage, bands[{y0,y1,from_ms,to_ms}], complete, sheet}` |
| | edit-decisions `captions.visible=false` (only on `yes`), a notice line |

## Acceptance
Fixtures with burned-in subtitles (AR, EN), with a lower-third only, with a static logo only, and clean footage:
`yes` / `no` / `no` / `no`; no false positive on lower-thirds or logos; the budget ends a long scan with
`complete: false` and no error; GPU and CPU decode give the same verdict.
