## K0. Clean-room rules (binding on every K task)
1. **Ideas and methods only.** No code, text, prompts, templates, presets, media, data tables, file names, style names
   or UI labels are copied from any audited or reviewed project. Implementers work from this addendum and our own
   measurements only; they never open the audited clone.
2. **Neutral names in the repo.** The repo (code, tests, specs, plans, commits, PROGRESS.md, release files) never names
   an audited project, its author or a reviewed vendor product. Use "an open-source tool" or "a reference tool". The
   names live only in the copy of this addendum outside the repo.
3. **Identity scan.** The audited project's identity strings (product, skill and author names, its environment variable
   and home-folder names, its move-family names, its Arabic UI labels and dialect triggers, its promotions) are added to
   the **plain list outside the repo**, and the HMAC hash file is regenerated by Task 19's local command (K-T0). Only the
   audited open-source project's identity strings are added: commercial vendors whose code we never read are not
   identity tokens (Addendum G already names them as a quality bar).
4. **Facts are re-derived, never copied.** Silence thresholds, loudness targets, safe-zone sizes, minimum text sizes,
   kashida positions and beat tolerances come from our own measurement or from primary sources (official platform
   pages, Unicode, typographic references), with the basis recorded next to each number.
5. **Licences of anything new.** Every new library, model or dataset is checked before it enters a lock or a download
   list: permissive or GPL-3.0-compatible, commercial use allowed, **no non-commercial terms, no AGPL, nothing paid**.
   The row goes into `docs/legal/third-party-licences.md` (name, version, licence, URL of the licence, commercial OK,
   attribution). A candidate that fails is replaced, never "used for now". (The MMS aligner exception in Addendum I is
   not extended to anything in K.)
6. **Every item:** tests written first and seen failing; an independent review (0 Critical, 0 Important); the full
   suite through heavy.sh; Mac parity tests on CI macOS; the live checks listed per task; a "Simplicity" line (G10:
   does it add a way to fail?).

### K-T1 Uncertain-word flags, risk first (item 1)
- **Behaviour.** After transcription, the engine ranks words to confirm by **risk × doubt**, so the editor checks the
  dangerous ones first:
  - risk classes: drug and product names (client glossary + a shipped medical lexicon + Latin or uncommon tokens in a
    medical clip), numbers, doses and units (Western and Arabic-Indic digits, spelled numbers such as «خمسمية», units
    such as mg/مجم/ml/وحدة), negations next to a claim («مش», «لا», «ما», "don't", "never"), durations and frequencies
    («مرتين في اليوم»);
  - doubt: low word probability (threshold measured on our transcripts, not guessed), decoder disagreement between
    chunks, or a Kalam correction that changed the word.
  - Output: a "check these first" block at the top of `subtitle-review.md`, the same list in `NEEDS-YOU.md` (T4.7) and in
    the panel data (Phase 6). A high-risk word not yet confirmed may not appear on screen. This is today's rule 4 of
    medical-accuracy, now enforced by `reel plan` (behind its K1 switch) with an error naming the word and its time.
  - **Confirming a word reuses today's flow; there is no new command.** The words-to-confirm list in
    `subtitle-review.md` is proofread, then recorded with `reel transcript-verify`, as the eval
    `transcript-words-to-confirm` checks. K-T1 only reorders that list and reads its confirmations.
  - **Step 1, the lexicon licence.** The shipped medical lexicon is built only from sources whose licence allows
    commercial redistribution, checked per K0.5. Known traps, excluded: DrugBank's open data (CC BY-NC), and
    RxNorm/UMLS content under the UMLS licence terms (restricted, needs a licence per user). Candidates are the client
    glossary, our own curated list, and public-domain or permissively licensed term lists, each recorded with its basis.
- **Acceptance.** On fixture transcripts (Arabic, English, mixed) with planted low-confidence doses, drug names and
  negations, every planted item ranks above every low-risk item; a confirmed word no longer blocks; no new false block
  on the existing transcript fixtures.
- **Tests first:** `test_safety_risk_words.py`: `test_dose_with_low_prob_ranks_first`, `test_arabic_indic_and_spelled_numbers`,
  `test_negation_next_to_claim_is_high_risk`, `test_unconfirmed_high_risk_word_blocks_plan`, `test_confirmed_word_unblocks`,
  `test_threshold_comes_from_config_with_basis`.
- **Phase:** 4 (parallel). **Size:** S. **Owner:** safety track (engine), executor (plan hook + skill text).

### K-T2 "What we removed" review clip (item 2)
- **Behaviour.** `reel removed-review <item>` renders a short MP4 of **only the parts the edit removes** (cuts, retakes,
  pauses removed by audio-clean, trimmed heads and tails), in timeline order. Each removed piece gets a one-second slate
  (source timecode, the removed words, the reason: pause / filler / retake / editor cut) and 0.5 s of kept context before
  and after, dimmed, so the editor hears the seam. Words whose loss changes meaning (negations, numbers, doses, drug
  names from K-T1) are highlighted on the slate and listed first in a text sheet next to the clip. GPU encode for this
  intermediate (T4.10), CPU fallback.
- **Acceptance.** A fixture where a «لا» and a dose fall inside a cut: both are on the slate and at the top of the sheet;
  duration = removed time + slates + context; the clip is viewed in the live check.
- **Tests first:** `test_safety_removed_review.py`: `test_lists_every_removed_span_in_order`, `test_meaning_words_highlighted`,
  `test_duration_formula`, `test_no_removed_parts_means_no_clip_and_a_plain_message`, `test_arabic_path_and_spaces`.
- **Live:** one PODCAST-TEST reel; the clip and sheet are viewed.
- **Phase:** 4 (parallel). **Size:** S. **Owner:** safety track.

### K-T3 Cut-boundary check after render (item 3)
- **Behaviour.** After the review render of a built project (today's preview-video; Phase 6's `render_review`),
  `reel seam-check <item>`:
  1. **force-aligns the expected words** (the words the plan keeps around each cut, ±4 s) against the **rendered**
     audio on the GPU. It always uses the **Apache-2.0 Arabic wav2vec2 aligner**, pinned, whatever G-ALIGN picks for
     transcription: K0.5 does not extend Addendum I's MMS exception to K. It is called through the tools track's
     `align_w2v*` public function with that model named, or loaded directly if that function cannot take a model choice.
     Forced alignment is used because a free re-transcription
     would disagree with the plan's words on clean cuts and break the 0-false-findings bar. A low-score expected word
     (lost or half word), or extra speech energy between two aligned words (a doubled word), is a finding. A free
     re-transcription of only that window is attached as evidence, never as the judge;
  2. measures an audio click at each cut (sample discontinuity and short high-band energy, thresholds measured on clean
     cuts);
  3. checks flash frames and one-frame leftovers at each cut (frame difference against both neighbours).

  **Short audio fades at every cut** become a build default. Each one is a fade of a few milliseconds on each side of
  every audio edit, its length measured as the shortest that removes clicks on our fixtures and recorded with the basis.
  - **Spike first.** Fades shorter than one frame through CEP/QE in Premiere are unproven. A spike on a copy shows
    whether a sub-frame audio transition can be set and read back. If it cannot, the fallback is a 1-frame audio
    crossfade, which is measured for clicks the same way.
  - **Ownership.** The builder hunk lives in `lib/ppro_build.py`, which the Adobe track owns (T4.1 fan-out). T4.1
    already adds start and end audio fades, so the Adobe track implements the per-cut fade **as an extension of T4.1's
    fade code**, never a second fade path. The safety track gives it the measured length and the click test.
  - The fade is listed in `docs/adobe/uxp-parity.md` "pending ops" for the UXP adapter.
- **Acceptance.** Fixtures with a planted doubled word, a lost word, a click and a one-frame leftover: each is found with
  its cut id and time. On 50 clean cuts from PODCAST-TEST: 0 false findings. Fades appear in the read-back.
- **Tests first:** `test_safety_seam.py`: `test_doubled_word_found`, `test_lost_word_found`, `test_click_found`,
  `test_flash_frame_found`, `test_clean_cuts_no_findings`, `test_alignment_is_judge_not_free_transcript`,
  `test_windows_merge_when_cuts_are_close`, `test_gpu_lock_respected_and_cpu_fallback`, `test_aligner_pinned_not_mms`.
  Builder (Adobe track,
  `test_t41*.py`): `test_audio_fade_each_edit_in_spec_and_readback`, `test_fade_switch_off_is_byte_identical`.
- **Relation:** T2.13's cut proof checks the cleaned audio before the build; K-T3 checks what Premiere actually rendered.
- **Phase:** 4 (parallel; the fade is behind its K1 switch, off until the Gate 4 tag and turned on in the first Phase 6
  commit after it). **Size:** M, plus the fade spike. **Owner:**
  safety track (seam check, fade length) + Adobe track (`ppro_build.py` fade as a T4.1 extension, CEP spike, UXP parity
  line in Phase 6).

### K-T5 Arabic integrity QC (item 5)
- **Behaviour.** Arabic letters join, so animating them one by one breaks the word. Rules:
  - kinetic text animates **whole words** (or whole lines), never letters, in Arabic and mixed lines; a Latin run inside
    an Arabic line follows the same unit;
  - a word is never split across lines or animation units (a check over the motion plan and the caption groups);
  - the After Effects read-back reports each text animator's unit ("based on words/lines"), and QC fails on a
    character-based unit for any layer with Arabic;
  - RTL order is verified on rendered frames (frame-inspector checklist, plus M2's frame check).
- **Acceptance.** A motion plan with a per-letter Arabic animation is refused with the scene id; a split word is
  refused; read-back of a built AE fixture reports the unit.
- **Tests first:** `test_safety_arabic_integrity.py`: `test_per_letter_arabic_refused`, `test_split_word_refused`,

### K-T6 Hook rules (item 6)
- **Behaviour.** `reel hook-check <item>` (also run inside `reel plan` and `reels-plan`):
  - 3–8 words (Arabic counted as words, with clitics «و/ف/ب/ال» attached, not separate words);
  - any number in the hook must exist in the transcript (digits normalised: Western, Arabic-Indic, spelled);
  - the hook is not simply the clip's first sentence repeated;

## K2. Differentiators (items 9–17, size M unless noted)

### K-T9 Signed decision log per reel (item 9; medicolegal audit trail)
- **Behaviour.** Every approval of on-screen wording is recorded, so the agency can show who approved which words and
  when:
  - `output/projects/<ID>/decisions/decision-log.jsonl`, append-only. Each entry: `seq`, `at` (UTC + local),
    `item`, `event_id`, `domain` (M1), `verdict`, `wording` (the exact on-screen text) and its sha256, the
    `plan_fingerprint`, `approver` (name and role as the editor enters them, e.g. "the doctor, by WhatsApp"),
    `recorded_by` (the editor from preferences), `via` (chat / panel), an optional evidence file (a screenshot of the
    approval message, copied into the folder), `prev_hash`, `signature`.
  - **Hash chain + signature:** each entry carries the sha256 of the previous entry and an ed25519 signature made with
    a per-machine key kept in the OS credential store (never in the repo or the client folder); the public key is in
    the log header.
  - `reel decisions <item>` lists; `reel decisions --verify` checks the chain and signatures; `reel decisions --export`
    writes an Arabic + English audit sheet (HTML → PDF, D16) for the client.
  - Recording is editor-only (`--by editor`, guard blocks subagents), like `assets-approve`.
- **Honest limit (stated in the guide):** the log proves the record was not changed after the fact on this machine; it
  does not stop someone with full control of the machine from writing a new log. It is an audit trail, not a legal
  signature.
- **Acceptance.** Edit, delete or reorder any entry → `--verify` fails and names the entry; a re-plan that changes a
  wording makes the old approval not apply to the new words; the export opens and reads right to left.
- **Tests first:** `test_decision_log.py`: `test_append_and_verify`, `test_tamper_edit_detected`, `test_tamper_delete_detected`,
  `test_reorder_detected`, `test_wording_change_needs_new_approval`, `test_subagent_cannot_record`,
  `test_key_from_credential_store_never_in_folder`, `test_export_rtl`.
- **Dependency:** the signing library goes through Task 12 (lock lines); the ed25519 primitive is the one E3 already
  uses where possible. **Used by:** M1 (one gate per domain).
- **Phase:** 6 (engine side, first Phase 6 engine task); panel view in Phase 6 (Adobe track). **Size:** M. **Owner:**
  executor.

### K-T13 Arabic kashida rules for After Effects text; whole-word animation (item 13)
- **Behaviour.** Kashida (ـ, U+0640 TATWEEL) lengthens a joining stroke to justify a line or add emphasis. Our rules
  come from Arabic typographic practice. **The letter classes are derived at build time from Unicode's
  `ArabicShaping.txt`** (pinned version, joining types R/D/U/T/C). They are never a hand-typed list. Examples: ا د ذ ر ز و
  and ة are R (right-joining only); ى is D (dual-joining); ء is U.
  - A kashida may go only after a **D** letter that, at that position, joins the letter after it (both letters are in
    the same joined run; T marks are skipped when deciding this).
  - Never after an R or U letter. Never inside the لا ligature or any required ligature.
  - **Never word-final.** This is a separate rule from the joining class: a final ى or ي (both D) still gets no kashida
    after it, because nothing follows inside the word.
  - never in a Latin run, a number, a word under three letters, or next to a letter carrying a mark where the mark
    would be detached;
  - at most one kashida position per word and a maximum elongation measured per font (it must look drawn, not
    stretched); positions ranked (a preference order we derive and test visually);
  - only on display and accent roles (hook, quote, key word), never on captions or body text (M3);
  - **Kashida animation is discrete, and it needs a spike first.** In After Effects, "growing a stroke" means stepping
    the number of U+0640 characters through Source Text keyframes (0 → 1 → 2 …). It is not a smooth tween. A
    feasibility spike on a copy decides whether the steps look intended at 25/30 fps in three OFL Arabic families, with
    a hold or an ease between steps. Only after a positive verdict does it become a motion move in the palette (K-T14),
    in whole-word units only (K-T5). If the verdict is negative, kashida stays a static display treatment, and the
    animation is recorded as not adopted.
- **Acceptance.** A rules table generated from `ArabicShaping.txt`, with tests per joining type. AE renders of fixture
  words in three OFL Arabic families, viewed by graphic-checker and the brand-systems-designer ("drawn, not stretched").
  The spike's verdict is recorded with its strips.
- **Tests first:** `test_kashida.py`: `test_classes_from_arabicshaping_pinned`, `test_r_and_u_letters_never`,
  `test_alef_maksura_is_dual_joining`, `test_word_final_never`, `test_lam_alef_never`, `test_latin_and_numbers_never`,
  `test_short_words_never`, `test_one_per_word`, `test_marks_stay_attached`, `test_captions_never_get_kashida`.
- **Phase:** 6 (with R-6.1/R-6.2 AE looks). **Size:** S–M for the static rules, plus a spike; the animation is sized
  after the spike. **Owner:** looks & motion track.

## K3. Beat detection and beat-synced montage (item 20 only)

### K-T20 Beat grid + montage on the beat
- **Behaviour.**
  - **Beat grid.** `reel beats <music-file>` computes tempo, beats and downbeats from a **licensed** track in
    `data/Music` (T4.6's licence file is required; a track without one is refused). The method is onset strength and
    tempo tracking, in our own code or in a permissive library chosen in step 1 (K0.5). Output `beats.json` holds the
    method, the tempo, the beat times and a confidence per section.
  - **Licence traps named in step 1, excluded:**
    - libraries whose pretrained model files are CC BY-NC-SA, even when their code is permissive (for example, the
      bundled models of some beat-tracking toolkits);
    - AGPL audio-analysis libraries.

    Each candidate's **code and model/data files** are checked separately.
  - **Montage.** A montage segment (an intro, an outro, a trailer for an episode, a B-roll run over the speaker's voice)
    cuts on beats: every 1, 2 or 4 beats by the music's energy and the shot's length; cuts are quantised to frames with
    no drift over the segment; speech is never cut for a beat (beats only move B-roll and title cuts); the build puts
    markers on beats.
  - **Motion videos:** scene changes snap to the nearest beat within a tolerance when a music bed is used. The snap lives
    in `lib/motion_timing.py`, which the executor owns, so the executor writes that hunk. The looks & motion track
    supplies the beat grid module (`lib/looks_beats.py`, under its `looks*.py`) and the tolerance data.
- **Acceptance.** Synthetic click tracks at 90/120/128/140 BPM: every beat within ±1 frame at 29.97 and 25 fps over
  60 s; three real licensed tracks: beats within ±40 ms of a hand-tapped reference; a Premiere build where every
  montage cut sits on a beat frame (read-back) and the review render is watched.
- **Tests first:** `test_beats.py`: `test_click_track_exact`, `test_tempo_change_sections`, `test_unlicensed_track_refused`,
  `test_frame_quantise_no_drift`, `test_speech_never_cut_for_beat`, `test_markers_in_spec`.
- **Phase:** 6 (engine + build; the AE snapping with the motion looks). **Size:** M–L. **Owner:** looks & motion track
  (beat grid `looks_beats.py`, montage rules data) + executor (`motion_timing.py` snap, `reel beats` CLI hunk) + Adobe
  track (Premiere beat markers and montage cuts in `ppro_build.py`).
